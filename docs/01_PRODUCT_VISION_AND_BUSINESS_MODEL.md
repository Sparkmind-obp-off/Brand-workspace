# Product Vision and Business Model

## Vision
Build a personal-brand operator that makes social marketing behave like an operating system: the owner supplies reality, expertise, offers, projects, and direction; the system turns those inputs into a continuous, measurable marketing machine.

## User Promise
**Give the system what you know and what you are building; it handles the repeatable marketing work while keeping strategic and risky decisions under your control.**

## Business Model
Brand Workspace can support two layers:

### 1. Internal Operator
Used by the owner to market their own services, products, projects, and personal brand. This is the first validation environment.

### 2. Productized Service / Platform
The same architecture can later operate multiple brands or clients with isolated workspaces, social connections, brand profiles, approvals, and analytics.

Potential monetization:
- Managed marketing service.
- Monthly operator subscription.
- Higher-tier multi-brand workspace.
- Setup/integration fees where appropriate.

## Value Creation
The system should optimize for outcomes rather than post volume:
- qualified attention
- conversations
- leads
- profile visits
- offer interest
- conversions
- repeatable content velocity

## Strategic Advantage
The moat is not text generation. Models are replaceable. The durable layer is the combination of:
- structured brand context
- reusable source material
- channel connectors
- publishing history
- analytics feedback
- operating procedures
- accumulated decision data

## Business Constraints
- Start with one owner and a small number of accounts.
- Prove actual publishing before building broad SaaS features.
- Never depend on an API approval that has not been granted.
- Keep provider-specific logic behind connectors.

## Success Metric
MVP success is one reliable end-to-end marketing loop that produces real published content and useful feedback, not the number of screens or agents implemented.
